The Hard Decisions

Decision 01

One Instance or Two?

The Situation Six months into the Bellami D2C + Pro build, the incoming CMO challenged the entire architecture — proposing two separate Shopify instances based on precedent from her previous brand.

What I did Built a decision matrix mapping pros and cons across maintenance, cost, customer experience, and supporting data.

Key Unlock Customer overlap data showed D2C and Pro weren't two separate groups — users moved between the user groups and it was critical that a unified customer view was created.

Decision 02

The Full Kitchen Sink

The Situation A copied version of Babe’s Pro verification flow was derailed by Shopify’s new SSO update — and the re-scoping conversation opened the door for net-new requests to sneak in, spiraling into a 100+ hour implementation.

What I Did Paused and returned to the initial PRD, specifically focusing on the “North Star” — what was the immediate need for this feature?

Key Unlock We found an agreeable MVP that followed Shopify’s SSO requirements, captured the information needed to tag a user as a stylist, and unlocked pro-pricing gating functionality.

Decision 02 · Artifact

Defining & re-defining the flow

FigJam iterations that captured the thinking and re-working of Pro verification — from early Pro vs Consumer branches through to the launch-ready SSO path.

Reflection

What I'd Do
Differently

01

Deeper discovery upfront — always

Discovery was the first thing compressed under timeline pressure across the early builds. In hindsight, a more structured discovery period regardless of timeline would have reduced decision friction downstream. This is now non-negotiable in how I approach new builds.

02

Stakeholder interviews across all brands, not just the combined build

User research was most thorough on the Bellami D2C + Pro combination. Earlier builds moved quickly and that meant some decisions were harder to defend without the customer data to back them up. Deeper insight across all three brands would have made those conversations easier and the decisions cleaner.

Example

A quiz feature was scoped, built, and never launched. We’d never aligned on what it was actually meant to solve — color matching, product type, texture? When we finally pulled customer service data and ran stakeholder interviews, there was still no consensus. The catalog was too complex for a simplified app.

We built the right solution to the wrong problem. It remains unlaunched to this day.

More structured discovery and stakeholder interviews upfront would have surfaced this before a single line of code was written. This is now non-negotiable in how I approach new builds.
